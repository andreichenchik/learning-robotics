State and discrete time

A minimal model of the loop that a robot actually executes on a computer.

State

State x is the minimal information about the present that, together with future inputs, is sufficient to predict the model's future.

Position is enough for a point controlled by velocity. A freely moving body usually needs position and velocity: the same position at different velocities leads to different futures.

One discrete transition

yₖ = xₖ₋d
eₖ = r − yₖ
uₖ = Kₚeₖ
xₖ₊₁ = xₖ + Δt · uₖ

k is the step number, Δt is the step duration, and d is the measurement delay in steps. Command uₖ is held between updates.

Without delay

eₖ₊₁ = (1 − KₚΔt)eₖ
0 < KₚΔt ≤ 1Convergence without overshoot.
1 < KₚΔt < 2Overshoots decay.
KₚΔt ≥ 2The error does not decay.
With delay, this simple boundary is no longer sufficient. The controller corrects a past state and can continue accelerating even after crossing the target.
