Uncertainty and covariance

Read a Gaussian state belief from its centre, marginal spreads, and coupled errors.

Belief and error

x ∼ N(μ, Σ)
e = x − μ

μ is the estimated centre. The actual error e is unknown; Σ describes the expected pattern of plausible errors.

Two-dimensional covariance

Σ = E[e eᵀ] = [ σx² σxy ]
[ σxy σy² ]
DiagonalMarginal variances; squared units.
Off-diagonalError co-variation; symmetric entries.
Standard deviationSquare root of variance; state units.

Covariance and correlation

σxy = ρ σx σy
−1 ≤ ρ ≤ 1
ρ > 0x and y errors tend to share a sign.
ρ = 0No linear co-variation; ellipse is axis-aligned.
ρ < 0x and y errors tend to oppose signs.

Ellipse interpretation

d² = (x − μ)ᵀ Σ⁻¹ (x − μ)

Constant d gives a Gaussian density contour. Eigenvectors of Σ give its directions; square roots of eigenvalues give principal standard deviations.

2D contourGaussian probability inside
d = 1≈ 39.3%
d = 2≈ 86.5%

For a 2D Gaussian, d² follows a chi-square distribution with two degrees of freedom. See the NIST definition and cumulative distribution.

Do not confuse these. Covariance is the estimator's modeled uncertainty, not its current unknown error. An ellipse is a probability contour, not a hard boundary.
Model boundary. One mean and covariance cannot represent multiple separated candidate locations. Gaussian localization requires a roughly unimodal belief.
