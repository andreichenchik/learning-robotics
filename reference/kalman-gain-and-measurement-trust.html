Kalman measurement correction

Combine a prediction with a noisy direct measurement using their variances.

Scalar correction

innovation = z − x̂⁻
K = P⁻ / (P⁻ + R)
x̂⁺ = x̂⁻ + K(z − x̂⁻)
P⁺ = (1 − K)P⁻
x̂⁻, P⁻Predicted mean and variance before the measurement.
z, RMeasured value and its error variance.
x̂⁺, P⁺Corrected mean and variance after the measurement.

Trust rule

P⁻ ≫ RK approaches 1; trust the measurement.
P⁻ = RK equals 0.5; split the innovation equally.
P⁻ ≪ RK approaches 0; trust the prediction.
Separate value from confidence. The innovation changes when z changes. The gain and corrected variance change only when P⁻ or R changes.

Matrix correction

y = z − Hx̂⁻
S = HP⁻Hᵀ + R
K = P⁻HᵀS⁻¹
x̂⁺ = x̂⁻ + Ky
P⁺ = (I − KH)P⁻

H maps the state into the quantity the sensor measures. S is the expected innovation covariance.

Assumptions

The standard Kalman correction assumes a correct linear measurement model and unbiased, independent Gaussian noise. Validate extreme innovations before accepting a measurement in a real robot.

Related lessons: Trust follows uncertainty and Prediction moves uncertainty. Primary sources: Dellaert & Hutchinson, Sensors for Mobile Robots; Kalman 1960.
