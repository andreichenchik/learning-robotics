# Mission: Robotics

## Why
Learn to build personal robots as a hobby: first explore mobile robots, manipulators, and other systems in simulation, then move on to a purposeful hardware project. Keep open the possibility of eventually turning this skill into a job or product without making that an immediate requirement of the learning process.

## Success looks like
- Independently model the motion, sensors, and behavior of simple robots in 2D and 3D.
- Explain why a chosen control, state-estimation, or planning algorithm works and where it stops working.
- Transfer behavior from a small model to a full simulator and later to suitable hardware.
- Complete several different simulation projects and use them to choose an area of interest.

## Constraints
- Only a MacBook and software simulators are available for now; it is too early to buy hardware.
- The learner has a background in mathematics and ML/DL, but it needs to be refreshed as required.
- The learning cadence is not fixed yet, so lessons must be short and self-contained.
- Practice must sustain interest, while theory must prevent future gaps in fundamentals.
- Every important concept must be encountered intuitively, formally, and in application.

## Out of scope
- Early specialization in a single class of robots.
- Buying hardware before there is a project with understood requirements.
- Studying ROS and complex infrastructure before they solve an actual need.
