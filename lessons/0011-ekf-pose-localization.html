Localization is the same loop over pose

Odometry predicts a robot's planar pose. A camera occasionally anchors that pose to the field. The extended Kalman filter keeps the familiar predict–correct loop when motion is nonlinear.

Outcome. You will predict one pose (x,y,θ), explain how heading uncertainty spreads into position, and use a camera pose measurement to shrink the covariance ellipse.

2. What localization estimates

x̂ = x positiony positionheading θ P = covariance of pose error

Localization estimates the robot's pose from its previous belief, executed motion, and sensor observations. We do not replay the entire history: the corrected pair (x̂⁺,P⁺) summarizes it for the next cycle.

Dellaert and Hutchinson define localization as recursively estimating robot position from the history of actions and observations; see Introduction to Robotics and Perception, 4.4: Localization.

3. One concrete curved motion

The robot begins at (0 m,0 m,0°). Odometry reports a 1 m movement while turning 30°. Using the heading halfway through the arc:

φ = θ + Δθ/2
x′ = x + d cos φ
y′ = y + d sin φ
θ′ = wrap(θ + Δθ)
x̂⁻ = 0.966 m0.259 m30°

The same forward distance produces different world-frame changes at different headings. The state update contains sin and cos, so there is no single constant matrix F that works everywhere.

4. “Extended” means linearize nearby errors

The EKF applies the nonlinear motion function f directly to the mean. For covariance, it recomputes a local influence matrix—the Jacobian—at the current pose:

x̂⁻ = f(x̂⁺,u)
P⁻ = F P⁺ Fᵀ + Q

F = ∂f/∂x evaluated at x̂⁺
F = 10−d sin φ01d cos φ001
Read one column physically. The third column says how a small heading error changes the next x, y, and θ. After moving, “pointing slightly wrong” becomes “ending at a slightly different position.”

5. Predict pose uncertainty

Start with position standard deviation 0.1 m and heading standard deviation 5°. Add process noise of 0.05 m and 2° for this motion. The prediction gives:

P⁻ ≈ 0.0130−0.0019−0.0020−0.00190.01960.0074−0.00200.00740.0088

The y variance is larger than the x variance because, at this heading, small direction errors mostly move the endpoint sideways. The off-diagonal values record the new coupling among position and heading errors.

6. A camera anchors pose to the field

Suppose known field tags let the camera report a noisy world-frame pose:

z = 1.020 m0.200 m28° H = I

Because the sensor directly reports all three state variables, H=I. The correction is the same Kalman correction as before, except the heading innovation must use the shortest wrapped angle:

y = [zx−x̂⁻, zy−ŷ⁻, wrap(zθ−θ̂⁻)]ᵀ
S = P⁻ + R
K = P⁻S⁻¹
x̂⁺ = x̂⁻ + Ky

For camera uncertainty σxy=0.15 m and σθ=8°, the corrected pose is approximately (0.988 m,0.227 m,28.9°). Its position variances fall from (0.0130,0.0196) to (0.0082,0.0099).

8. The EKF localization algorithm

repeat for each odometry increment u:
x̂⁻ = f(x̂⁺, u)
F = Jacobian of f at x̂⁺ and u
P⁻ = F P⁺ Fᵀ + Q

if camera pose z arrives:
y = z − x̂⁻; wrap yθ
S = P⁻ + R
K = P⁻S⁻¹
x̂⁺ = x̂⁻ + Ky; wrap θ̂⁺
P⁺ = (I − K)P⁻
else:
x̂⁺, P⁺ = x̂⁻, P⁻

9. Model boundary

This lesson assumes the initial pose is roughly known, the field map and tag poses are known, and one Gaussian is a useful local description. The EKF can fail after a large initialization error, a false camera pose, unmodeled bias, or delayed measurements. If the robot could occupy several widely separated places, a grid or particle filter can represent that multimodal belief; if the map is also unknown, the problem becomes SLAM.

For a practical pose-estimator architecture, see WPILib: Pose Estimators: encoder and gyro updates arrive periodically, while field-relative vision poses are added occasionally with tunable measurement uncertainty and timestamps.
