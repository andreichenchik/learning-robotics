External pose corrections

Odometry follows rapid local motion but drifts. An occasional world-referenced measurement can pull its estimate back—while also injecting measurement noise.

Outcome. You will apply one predict–correct cycle, tune a correction weight, and explain why trusting either odometry or an absolute sensor completely can fail.

1. Two imperfect sources

Wheel odometry predicts how pose changes from one instant to the next. Because it integrates increments, its errors accumulate. A camera observing known markers, motion capture, or another localization system can instead produce a pose in the world frame. That measurement does not accumulate encoder drift, but each reading is noisy.

OdometryFrequent and locally smooth; its error grows over time.
Absolute poseAnchored to the world; individual readings can jump.
Combined estimatePredict continuously; correct when a measurement arrives.

Robotics example: Introduction to Robotics and Perception, 7.3: Sensing for Drones contrasts high-frequency relative motion with lower-frequency absolute pose measurements.

2. Predict, compare, correct

Wheel increments
Predict q̂⁻
Measure z
Correct q̂⁺

The superscript minus denotes the prediction before using the new external measurement. The superscript plus denotes the corrected estimate.

innovation = z − q̂⁻
q̂⁺ = q̂⁻ + α · innovation

The innovation is new information: the discrepancy between what the external sensor reports and what odometry predicted. The correction weight α decides how much of that discrepancy to apply.

3. The weight expresses trust

α = 0Ignore the measurement. The estimate remains pure odometry.
0 < α < 1Move partway toward the measurement.
α = 1Accept the measurement completely, including its noise.

A small weight produces smoother motion but corrects drift slowly. A large weight removes drift quickly but makes the estimate follow noisy readings. A fixed α is a deliberate simplification; later, uncertainty will determine the weight.

4. Angles need wrapped differences

Position coordinates can be subtracted directly. Heading cannot: 179° and −179° are only 2° apart, not 358°.

eθ = wrap(zθ − θ̂⁻)
θ̂⁺ = wrap(θ̂⁻ + α · eθ)

Always correct through the shortest signed angular difference.

6. What this model leaves out

Real pose estimators do not normally use one arbitrary, fixed weight for every coordinate and every measurement. They track uncertainty, can assign different trust to position and heading, compensate for sensor latency, and reject implausible measurements.

Primary practical source: WPILib: Pose Estimators. It shows production estimators combining periodic odometry with occasional field-relative pose measurements and tuning their relative trust through measurement uncertainty.
