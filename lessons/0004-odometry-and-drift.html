Odometry and drift

A robot cannot read its true pose directly. It can, however, measure wheel rotation and repeatedly estimate how its pose changed.

Outcome. You will derive differential-drive odometry, compare an estimated pose with ground truth, and explain why small encoder errors accumulate into drift.

1. Truth is not a sensor reading

In a simulator we can inspect the true pose q=(x,y,θ). A physical robot does not receive that value for free. Its controller maintains an estimate, written q̂, from available measurements.

Odometry is this repeated estimation of chassis motion from wheel motion. It is local: each update starts from the previous estimate, not from an external map.

Primary source: Modern Robotics, 13.4: Odometry.

2. From encoder ticks to distance

An encoder measures a wheel's angular increment. With wheel radius r, angular motion becomes travel along the ground:

ΔsL = r · ΔφL
ΔsR = r · ΔφR

The odometry update only needs the left and right travel since the previous reading. Absolute tick counts are useful because subtracting consecutive counts produces these increments.

3. From wheel travel to chassis motion

The average wheel travel moves the chassis forward. Their difference rotates it:

Δs = (ΔsR + ΔsL) / 2
Δθ = (ΔsR − ΔsL) / L

Here L is the track width. If both wheels report the same travel, Δθ=0. If the right wheel reports more travel, the estimate turns left.

4. Integrating the estimate

For a short interval, use the heading halfway through the turn. This midpoint update is more accurate than applying the whole translation at the old heading:

θmid = θ̂ₖ + Δθ/2
x̂ₖ₊₁ = x̂ₖ + Δs cos θmid
ŷₖ₊₁ = ŷₖ + Δs sin θmid
θ̂ₖ₊₁ = wrap(θ̂ₖ + Δθ)

The key word is integrating: every new estimate depends on the previous estimate. An error is therefore carried into all later updates.

6. Bias and noise fail differently

Scale biasA repeatable proportional error, such as one encoder reporting 5% too much travel.
NoiseA varying measurement error whose individual samples may be positive or negative.
DriftThe growing pose error produced by integrating imperfect increments.

A wheel mismatch first corrupts Δθ. The wrong heading then rotates every later forward increment in the wrong direction, coupling orientation error into position error.

Model boundary. This lab changes encoder measurements while keeping true wheel motion ideal. Real robots also slip, skid, deform tyres, encounter uneven floors, and use imperfect wheel radius and track-width calibration.

Engineering reference: WPILib: Differential Drive Odometry. Its documentation likewise warns that encoder- and gyro-based pose estimates drift over time.
